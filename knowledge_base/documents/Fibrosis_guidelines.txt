Pulmonary fibrosis is a chronic interstitial process marked by architectural distortion and increased linear or reticular opacities in the lung parenchyma. Radiographically, fibrosis may appear as reticular opacities, traction bronchiectasis, volume loss, and a lower-lung-predominant pattern in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis or as more diffuse changes in other fibrotic disorders. The pattern can be subtle early on and progressively more pronounced with advanced disease.

The differential diagnosis includes chronic pneumonitis, postinfectious scarring, radiation fibrosis, and pulmonary edema. Findings such as traction bronchiectasis and subpleural basilar predominance help support fibrotic change rather than simple inflammation or edema. In some cases, scarcity of pleural effusion or lack of diffuse alveolar opacities can help distinguish fibrosis from acute lung injury.

Recommended next steps typically include clinical history, pulmonary function testing, and review of exposure history, autoimmune disease, and prior chest radiation. High-resolution CT is often useful for confirming a fibrotic pattern and determining whether the process is typical or requires biopsy and specialist evaluation.
