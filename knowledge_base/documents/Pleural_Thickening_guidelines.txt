Pleural thickening is an abnormal increase in pleural density or pleural rind seen on chest imaging. On chest radiographs, it often appears as a smooth or irregular pleural line that may be unilateral or bilateral and can accompany thickened fissures or adjacent pleural fluid. It may reflect prior inflammation, asbestos exposure, chronic pleuritis, or malignancy, and can be difficult to distinguish from peripheral fibrosis or scarring without cross-sectional imaging.

The differential diagnosis includes pleural effusion, passive atelectasis, chest wall lesions, and adjacent pulmonary fibrosis. Smooth pleural thickening may be more benign or chronic, while nodular or irregular pleural thickening raises concern for malignancy or pleural disease. Clinical exposure history, including asbestos or prior chest surgery, is important.

Recommended workup often involves chest CT to define the extent, loculation, and relationship to the lung and chest wall. If pleural thickening is nodular or associated with effusion, malignant pleural disease or prior inflammatory processes should be considered, and further biopsy or thoracic consultation may be warranted.
