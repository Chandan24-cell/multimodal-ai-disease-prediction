Atelectasis is a loss of lung volume caused by alveolar collapse or reduced inflation. On chest radiography, it commonly appears as plate-like or linear opacity, increased density in the affected lung base, and, when significant, elevation of the hemidiaphragm or shift of fissures toward the collapsed segment. Subsegmental atelectasis may be subtle and can mimic pneumonia or pleural fluid if volume loss is not appreciated.

The main differential diagnosis includes lower-lobe consolidation, pleural effusion, and focal pneumonia. Clinical correlation is essential because atelectasis can be caused by mucus plugging, postoperative states, diaphragmatic splinting, or obstructive lesions. When present, associated findings such as small airway obstruction or elevated hemidiaphragm help support the diagnosis.

Recommended workup usually includes review of the patient history, symptom acuity, and recent surgery or immobility. A focused chest radiograph may be repeated after incentive spirometry or bronchodilator therapy when clinically appropriate, while persistent or asymmetric atelectasis warrants further evaluation for obstructing endobronchial lesions, malignancy, or occult infection.
