A pulmonary nodule is a rounded opacity measuring less than 3 cm, often seen incidentally on chest imaging. Typical radiographic features include a small, circumscribed lesion with variable density and, if calcified, a benign pattern such as popcorn or dense central calcification. A smooth margin and stable size over time are reassuring, whereas irregular margins, spiculation, or growth raise concern.

The differential diagnosis includes granuloma, infectious focus, previous inflammatory scar, primary lung cancer, and metastatic disease. Characterization depends on size, density, location, and interval growth; solitary nodules require careful comparison with prior imaging. Some nodules represent benign infectious granulomas, while others may be early malignancies.

Recommended workup generally includes comparison with prior imaging and CT for nodule morphology, density, and size. If the lesion is indeterminate or enlarging, a management algorithm incorporating risk factors, follow-up interval imaging, or biopsy is appropriate.
