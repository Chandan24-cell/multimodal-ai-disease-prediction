Cardiomegaly is radiographic enlargement of the cardiac silhouette beyond expected normal limits relative to thoracic width. On a frontal chest radiograph, the heart may appear enlarged with widened cardiothoracic ratio, increased central vascular prominence, or a globular silhouette in cases of severe chamber enlargement. Cardiomegaly is a finding rather than a specific diagnosis and can arise from volume overload, chronic hypertension, valvular disease, ischemic cardiomyopathy, or pericardial disease.

The differential diagnosis includes physiologic variation, chest wall deformity, obesity, and technical magnification factors. A true enlarged cardiac silhouette should be interpreted together with clinical context and the presence of pulmonary vascular congestion, pleural effusions, or pulmonary edema. Marked enlargement without pulmonary findings can also reflect chronic cardiomyopathy or prior cardiac surgery.

Recommended next steps include bedside assessment of symptoms and signs of heart failure, review of prior imaging, and echocardiography when clinically indicated. If cardiomegaly is associated with pulmonary vascular redistribution, edema, or pleural effusions, the workup should progress toward heart failure evaluation, volume status assessment, and management of underlying cardiac disease.
