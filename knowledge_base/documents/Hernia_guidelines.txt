A diaphragmatic hernia is the protrusion of abdominal contents into the thoracic cavity through a defect or weakness in the diaphragm. On chest radiographs, this may appear as a focal lucent area above the diaphragm, bowel gas pattern in the thorax, or displaced mediastinal structures depending on the hernia type and size. Morgagni and Bochdalek hernias are among the classic congenital forms, while hiatal hernias are frequently seen on the subdiaphragmatic region and may be more subtle on a standard upright chest view.

The differential diagnosis includes bowel in the thorax from trauma, eventration of the diaphragm, cystic lung lesions, and congenital diaphragmatic abnormalities. Clinical suspicion is important because some hernias are asymptomatic while others produce respiratory compromise, bowel obstruction, or vomiting. Small hernias can be overlooked, especially if the stomach bubble or bowel gas pattern is not recognized.

Recommended workup often includes a focused chest or abdominal radiograph and, when uncertainty remains, CT or upper GI contrast evaluation. Surgical evaluation is warranted when symptoms, incarceration, or volvulus is suspected, especially in acute presentations.
