Pneumonia is an infection causing alveolar inflammation and replacement of air with inflammatory exudate. Radiographically, it often appears as focal or multifocal airspace opacity with or without air bronchograms, and lobar involvement may be seen in typical bacterial pneumonia. The pattern can vary by organism, host immune status, and aspiration risk, making radiographic appearance a clue rather than a definitive diagnosis.

The differential diagnosis includes atelectasis, pulmonary edema, pulmonary embolism, organizing pneumonia, and lung cancer with postobstructive consolidation. Features such as lobar distribution, air bronchograms, and a more homogeneous opacity support pneumonia, whereas diffuse interstitial opacities or vascular redistribution may suggest alternative diagnoses. Clinical symptoms such as fever, cough, and dyspnea should be correlated with the image.

Recommended workup includes clinical assessment, oxygen status, and targeted laboratory and microbiologic evaluation when clinically indicated. If the patient is hospitalized or not improving, repeat imaging and consideration of CT, bronchoscopy, or alternative diagnoses such as abscess or malignancy are appropriate.
