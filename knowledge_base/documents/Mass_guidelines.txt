A pulmonary mass is a rounded or irregular opacity that is at least 3 cm in diameter and may represent a primary lung neoplasm, metastatic lesion, or focal infection. On chest radiography, a mass usually appears as a well-defined or irregular lesion that may be associated with lobar atelectasis, pleural effusion, hilar adenopathy, or adjacent soft-tissue density. The margin and location can provide clues, but imaging alone often cannot distinguish benign from malignant lesions.

The differential diagnosis includes pneumonia, organizing pneumonia, lung abscess, large granuloma, and less commonly focal fibrosis or infarct. A spiculated lesion or associated adenopathy raises concern for malignancy, whereas a smooth and well-defined lesion may be more consistent with a benign process. Clinical context, smoking history, and prior imaging are crucial.

Recommended workup typically includes chest CT for better characterization, evaluation of local invasion, and staging of mediastinal or hilar disease. If suspicious, tissue diagnosis through bronchoscopy, biopsy, or PET-CT may be warranted depending on location and patient factors.
